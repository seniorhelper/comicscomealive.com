Meet the real Fluffles

Every great comic character starts somewhere. Fluffles started in a real cage, on a real green ramp, munching real greens out of a lime green bowl. That curious little face, the shiny black coat, the white belt around the middle and the white stripe across the top of the head became the cartoon you see in every episode.

In the comics, Fluffles lives with best buddy Biscuit and a kind family we call the Tall Ones. Every day brings a new mystery: a crinkly bag, a dark tunnel, a first snowflake, a vacuum monster. And every mystery ends with something true about how guinea pigs live, play and stay healthy.

The cast

Fluffles

A smooth black guinea pig with a white belt and a white headband stripe. Curious about everything. Brave, mostly. Wheeks loudly at the snack door.

Biscuit

Fluffles' best buddy: a fluffy ginger-and-white guinea pig with swirly tufts. Loves naps, hay and facts. Always kind, always calm.

Dot

A cheerful ladybug who lives in the garden outside the window and tells Fluffles all about the big world outside.

The Tall Ones

The loving family who takes care of Fluffles and Biscuit. We only ever see their hands and sock feet, usually holding snacks.

Captain Kapow

The caped hero of Comics Come Alive drops in for a special guest episode, and learns what Fluffles' superpower is.

Fluffles Facts: real guinea pig facts for kids

Every episode ends with one true fact. Here they all are in one place. Thinking about a guinea pig of your own? These are a great start, and your vet is the best guide.

  • Meet the Real FlufflesGuinea pigs are not pigs, and they do not come from Guinea! They are rodents, also called cavies, and their wild cousins live in the grassy Andes mountains of South America. Read episode 1
  • The Crinkly BagWhen a guinea pig hears a sound that means food, like a crinkly bag of hay, it often lets out a loud, happy squeal called a "wheek." Read episode 2
  • The Vitamin C CaseGuinea pigs cannot make their own vitamin C, just like people. They need some every day from fresh veggies like bell peppers, plus good guinea pig pellets. Read episode 3
  • A Buddy Named BiscuitGuinea pigs are social herd animals, and most are happier with a guinea pig buddy. New friends should meet slowly, on neutral ground, with a grown-up helping. Read episode 4
  • The Mysterious TunnelIn the wild, guinea pigs are prey animals, so they feel safest when they have places to hide. Every guinea pig should have its own hidey house or tunnel. Read episode 5
  • Popcorn Lessons"Popcorning" is when a happy guinea pig suddenly hops straight up, sometimes twisting in the air. It usually means they feel safe and excited. Read episode 6
  • The Vacuum MonsterGuinea pigs startle at loud noises. When they are scared they may freeze or dash to a hiding spot, so give them a calm, quiet place to live. Read episode 7
  • The First SnowflakeGuinea pigs are indoor pets. They are most comfortable around 60 to 80 degrees F (16 to 27 C) and should be kept away from cold drafts, heaters and hot sun. Read episode 8
  • The Cucumber CaperCucumber is mostly water, so it is fine as a small snack sometimes. Leafy greens and veggies are everyday foods, but hay is always the main meal. Read episode 9
  • The Vet VisitA guinea pig's front teeth never stop growing! Chewing lots of hay keeps them worn down, and a yearly vet checkup helps keep teeth healthy. Read episode 10
  • The Box Was BetterPlain cardboard boxes, with no tape, staples or ink, make great free hideouts and chew toys. Guinea pigs love hidey spots with two doors. Read episode 11
  • The Shiny WheelExercise wheels and rolling balls are not safe for guinea pigs, because their backs are not built for them. Instead they need a roomy cage and safe floor time to run. Read episode 12
  • Picnic PickSome foods are never safe for guinea pigs, like chocolate, onions, garlic, avocado and potatoes. Always check with a vet or a trusted care guide before sharing a new food. Read episode 13
  • The Other Guinea PigA guinea pig's eyes are on the sides of its head, so it can see almost all the way around, but not right under its own nose. Its whiskers help it feel what is close by. Read episode 14
  • Midnight MunchiesGuinea pigs do not sleep all night like you. They take lots of short naps, day and night, and sometimes they even nap with their eyes open! Read episode 15
  • The Great Weigh-InWeighing a guinea pig once a week on a kitchen scale is an easy way to spot health problems early. A sudden drop in weight is a reason to call the vet. Read episode 16
  • The Hay FortHay should make up most of a guinea pig's diet and be available all day long. It is great for their tummies and their teeth. Read episode 17
  • Brush DayA low, soft purr with a relaxed body usually means a guinea pig is content. Smooth-coated guinea pigs need only gentle brushing now and then; long-haired ones need it every day. Read episode 18
  • The Wheek ConcertGuinea pigs learn the sounds of their daily routine. Many wheek loudly when they hear the fridge open or a veggie bag rustle, because snack time might be coming. Read episode 19
  • Dot at the WindowGuinea pigs love fresh grass, but it should come from a yard with no weed killer or chemicals, be rinsed first, and never come from a lawn mower bag. Read episode 20
  • Gotcha DayGuinea pigs can live 5 to 7 years, and some live even longer. That is a lot of Gotcha Days, so caring for one is a big and happy promise. Read episode 21
  • Where Should Fluffles Explore?Guinea pigs chew almost everything, including electrical cords. Floor time should always be supervised, in a safe space with no wires or gaps to squeeze into. Read episode 22
  • The ThunderstormGuinea pigs feel braver together. During loud storms or fireworks, a buddy, a hidey house and a calm, quiet room help them feel safe. Read episode 23
  • Fluffles Meets Captain KapowGuinea pigs have a real-life superpower: baby guinea pigs, called pups, are born with fur, open eyes and teeth, and they can run around just hours after they are born! Read episode 24

Questions about Fluffles

Is Fluffles a real guinea pig?

Yes! The series is inspired by a real guinea pig named Fluffles: a smooth-coated black guinea pig with a white band around the middle and a white stripe on top of the head. The real photo opens the series, and the cartoon Fluffles is drawn to match.

What age is The Adventures of Fluffles for?

It is made for kids about 3 to 9 and the grown-ups who read with them, but anyone who loves animals can enjoy it. Every story is gentle, positive and funny, with no scary or mean moments.

Are the guinea pig facts real?

Yes. Each episode ends with a Fluffles Fact based on guinea pig care guidance from animal welfare groups and veterinarians, such as needing vitamin C every day, unlimited hay, and a guinea pig buddy. For your own pet, your vet is always the best source.

Why do the episodes look so different from each other?

On purpose! Some episodes are old-school black-and-white newspaper strips, some are big full-color Sunday pages, some are modern vertical-scroll webtoons, and some are animated motion comics, silent comics or choose-a-panel comics where you pick what happens.

Is the Fluffles comic free?

Yes. Every episode is free to read on any phone, tablet or computer, with no account and no download.

Can kids read it on their own?

Many can! The lettering is big and clear, and every episode has a transcript you can open below the comic, which is handy for reading aloud together or for screen readers.
